Pediatric Fever, Dehydration, and Red Flags (WHO / NIH MedlinePlus)

Evaluating illness in infants and children:
Young children, especially infants under 3 months of age, have vulnerable immune systems and can deteriorate rapidly.

Immediate emergency warning signs in children:
- Fever in any infant under 3 months of age (temperature of 100.4°F / 38°C or higher): Requires immediate hospital assessment to rule out serious bacterial infection.
- Lethargy or unresponsiveness: Difficulty waking the child, lack of eye contact, or absence of response to parents.
- Respiratory distress: Grunting, nasal flaring, chest indrawing (intercostal retractions), or breathing more than 50-60 breaths per minute.
- Pale, mottled, ashen, or blue skin (cyanosis).
- Seizures (convulsions) or neck stiffness with high fever.
- Non-blanching purpuric or petechial rash (does not fade under pressure from a clear drinking glass).

Signs of severe dehydration:
- Absence of wet diapers for more than 6-8 hours.
- Sunken eyes or sunken fontanelle (soft spot on infant's head).
- Crying without tears.
- Extreme dry mouth, cracked lips, or skin that stays tented when pinched.
- Fast breathing and cold extremities.

What to do:
Seek same-day clinical assessment or emergency care at a pediatric hospital. Administer small, frequent sips of Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS) while traveling to medical care.

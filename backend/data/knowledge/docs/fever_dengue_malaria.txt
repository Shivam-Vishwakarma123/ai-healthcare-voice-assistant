Fever, Dengue, and Vector-Borne Diseases Guidance (WHO / MOHFW India NVBDCP)

Understanding acute fever in tropical and subtropical regions:
Fever is a normal immune response to infection, but persistent or high fever requires clinical evaluation, especially during seasonal vector-borne disease outbreaks (Dengue, Malaria, Chikungunya, Typhoid).

Dengue Fever warning signs:
Dengue virus infection presents with sudden high fever (104°F / 40°C), severe retro-orbital (behind the eye) headache, severe muscle and joint pain ("breakbone fever"), nausea, and rash.
Critical warning signs of Severe Dengue (usually appearing around day 3-7 when fever subsides):
- Severe persistent abdominal pain or tenderness.
- Persistent vomiting (inability to retain fluids).
- Bleeding from mucosal membranes (gums, nose, blood in vomit or stools).
- Extreme lethargy, restlessness, or sudden dizziness upon standing.
- Rapid breathing or cold, clammy skin.

Malaria:
Malaria presents with cyclic high fever, shivering chills (rigors), profuse sweating, and fatigue. Diagnostic confirmation via rapid diagnostic test (RDT) or blood smear microscopy is required before initiating targeted antimalarial medication.

Safe home care and precautions:
- Maintain generous oral fluid intake with ORS (Oral Rehydration Solution), tender coconut water, clean water, and light broths.
- Avoid aspirin, ibuprofen, and other NSAIDs (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs) because they increase the risk of hemorrhage in dengue.
- Seek same-day clinical assessment and complete blood count (CBC) monitoring. If warning signs of bleeding, severe abdominal pain, or confusion appear, seek emergency care immediately.

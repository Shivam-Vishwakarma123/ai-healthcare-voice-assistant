Anaphylaxis and Severe Allergic Reactions (NIH MedlinePlus)

What is anaphylaxis?
Anaphylaxis is a severe, rapidly progressing, and life-threatening systemic allergic reaction. It typically occurs within minutes of exposure to an allergen (e.g., insect stings, medications, peanuts, seafood, or latex).

Clinical signs of anaphylaxis:
- Airway and breathing: Swelling of the tongue, lips, throat, or uvula; hoarseness or difficulty swallowing; shortness of breath, wheezing, or stridor.
- Skin and mucous membranes: Generalized hives (urticaria), intense itching, flushing, or sudden swelling under the skin (angioedema).
- Cardiovascular: Sudden drop in blood pressure (hypotension), feeling faint, weak rapid pulse, or loss of consciousness.
- Gastrointestinal: Severe crampy abdominal pain, sudden vomiting, or diarrhea.

Emergency management:
- Anaphylaxis is a true emergency. If an epinephrine auto-injector is available, administer it immediately into the outer mid-thigh.
- Call emergency services immediately (dial 112 or 108 in India).
- Lay the person flat with their legs elevated unless they are vomiting or having severe breathing difficulty (in which case allow them to sit upright).
- Do not attempt to give oral antihistamines or fluids if the person is struggling to breathe or losing consciousness.

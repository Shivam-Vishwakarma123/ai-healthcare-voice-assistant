Hypertension and Hypertensive Crisis Guidelines (NIH MedlinePlus / NHLBI)

Understanding high blood pressure:
Hypertension is generally defined as a sustained systolic blood pressure of 130 mm Hg or higher, or diastolic blood pressure of 80 mm Hg or higher. Often called a "silent killer," uncomplicated hypertension rarely causes obvious symptoms until organ damage occurs.

Hypertensive Crisis:
A hypertensive crisis occurs when blood pressure spikes rapidly, typically exceeding 180 systolic and/or 120 diastolic.
- Hypertensive Urgency: Blood pressure > 180/120 mm Hg without signs of immediate target-organ damage. Requires same-day clinical evaluation and medication adjustment.
- Hypertensive Emergency: Blood pressure > 180/120 mm Hg with acute target-organ damage.
Signs of hypertensive emergency include:
- Severe acute chest pain or pressure.
- Severe throbbing headache accompanied by confusion or altered mental state.
- Blurred vision or visual loss.
- Sudden shortness of breath.
- Nausea and vomiting.
- Seizures or neurological deficits.

Emergency Action:
If blood pressure is above 180/120 and any symptom of organ damage is present, call 112 or 108 immediately. Do not delay emergency hospital care.

Stroke Symptoms and Emergency FAST Recognition (NIH MedlinePlus / NINDS)

What is a stroke?
A stroke occurs when blood flow to an area of the brain is interrupted (ischemic stroke) or when a blood vessel in the brain ruptures (hemorrhagic stroke). Brain cells begin to die within minutes, making stroke an absolute time-critical emergency.

Recognizing stroke using the F.A.S.T. test:
- F = Face Drooping: Does one side of the face droop or feel numb? Ask the person to smile. Is the smile uneven or lopsided?
- A = Arm Weakness: Is one arm weak or numb? Ask the person to raise both arms. Does one arm drift downward?
- S = Speech Difficulty: Is speech slurred, garbled, or difficult to understand? Ask the person to repeat a simple sentence.
- T = Time to Call Emergency Services: If the person shows any of these symptoms, call 112 or 108 immediately. Check the time so clinicians know when the first symptoms appeared.

Additional warning signs:
- Sudden numbness or weakness of the face, arm, or leg, especially on one side of the body.
- Sudden confusion, trouble speaking or understanding speech.
- Sudden vision problems in one or both eyes.
- Sudden severe dizziness, loss of balance, or difficulty walking.
- Sudden severe headache ("thunderclap" headache) with no known cause.

Every minute counts: Thrombolytic (clot-dissolving) therapy or endovascular intervention is most effective when administered within the first 3 to 4.5 hours of symptom onset.

Breathing Difficulty and Respiratory Warning Signs (NIH MedlinePlus)

What causes breathing difficulty (dyspnea)?
Breathing difficulty is a subjective sensation of not getting enough air. It ranges from mild breathlessness during physical exertion to acute life-threatening respiratory failure.

Common causes:
- Asthma or Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) exacerbation.
- Lower respiratory tract infections including pneumonia and bronchitis.
- Heart conditions including congestive heart failure and cardiac ischemia.
- Pulmonary embolism (blood clot in the lungs) or pneumothorax (collapsed lung).
- Severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) causing swelling of airways.

When to seek immediate emergency care:
Seek immediate emergency medical attention (dial 112 or 108 in India) if shortness of breath:
- Begins suddenly or is severe and worsening.
- Is accompanied by chest pain, pressure, or tightness.
- Causes blue, grey, or pale lips, fingernails, or skin (cyanosis).
- Interferes with the ability to speak in full sentences.
- Produces stridor, high-pitched whistling, or severe wheezing.
- Is accompanied by confusion, fainting, or sudden drowsiness.

Routine or non-emergency situations:
Mild breathlessness that improves with rest and without chest pain or fever should be evaluated by a healthcare provider or pulmonologist for definitive diagnosis and lung function testing.

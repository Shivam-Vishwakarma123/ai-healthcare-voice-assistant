Diabetes Acute Complications: Hyperglycemia and Hypoglycemia (NIH MedlinePlus / NIDDK)

Overview of blood sugar emergencies:
Diabetes mellitus requires careful balance of nutrition, physical activity, and prescribed medication (insulin or oral hypoglycemic agents). Extreme shifts in blood glucose levels can be life-threatening.

Hypoglycemia (Low blood sugar, typically < 70 mg/dL):
- Symptoms: Trembling or shakiness, sweating, rapid heartbeat (palpitations), dizziness, extreme hunger, anxiety, confusion, slurred speech, or blurred vision.
- Immediate action (Rule of 15): If conscious and able to swallow, consume 15 grams of fast-acting carbohydrate (e.g., 3-4 glucose tablets, half a cup of fruit juice, or 1 tablespoon of sugar/honey). Recheck blood sugar after 15 minutes.
- If unconscious or having seizures: Do NOT give oral liquids. Call emergency services (112 / 108) immediately.

Hyperglycemia and Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA):
- Symptoms: Extreme thirst (polydipsia), very frequent urination (polyuria), nausea and persistent vomiting, abdominal pain, fruity-smelling breath, deep rapid breathing (Kussmaul breathing), and confusion or drowsiness.
- Action: DKA is a medical emergency requiring intravenous fluids, electrolyte management, and insulin in a hospital environment.

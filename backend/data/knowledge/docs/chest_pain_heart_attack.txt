Chest Pain and Heart Attack Warning Signs (NIH MedlinePlus / NHLBI)

What are the warning signs of a heart attack?
A heart attack (myocardial infarction) occurs when blood flow to a part of the heart is blocked. Chest pain or discomfort is the most common symptom, but other symptoms can also occur.

Key symptoms of a heart attack:
- Chest pain or discomfort: Discomfort, pressure, fullness, squeezing, or aching in the center or left side of the chest that lasts for more than a few minutes or goes away and comes back.
- Radiating pain: Discomfort or pain spreading to one or both arms (especially the left arm), back, neck, jaw, or upper stomach.
- Shortness of breath: Feeling breathless, which may occur before or along with chest discomfort.
- Cold sweat, nausea, lightheadedness: Sudden cold sweating, dizziness, unexplained nausea, or vomiting.

Women, older adults, and individuals with diabetes may have atypical symptoms, including sudden profound fatigue, breathlessness, indigestion-like abdominal discomfort, or dizziness without severe chest pressure.

Emergency Action:
Chest pain accompanied by pressure, breathlessness, or radiating discomfort is a medical emergency. Immediately call national emergency services (in India, dial 112 or 108 for ambulance) and go to the nearest emergency-capable hospital. Do not attempt to drive yourself.
